DOCUMENT TITLE: Modern NLP Architectures and Semantic Representation
MODULE: Natural Language Processing (Semester VII)

1. The Transformer Paradigm
The Transformer architecture was introduced in the seminal 2017 paper "Attention Is All You Need" by Ashish Vaswani and colleagues at Google Brain and Google Research. Prior to Transformers, state-of-the-art NLP relied primarily on Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks. The fundamental computational bottleneck of recurrent architectures was their strictly sequential processing, which prevented parallelization across time steps and suffered from gradient vanishing over long-distance dependencies.

Transformers eliminated recurrence completely, relying entirely on Multi-Head Self-Attention mechanisms. In a multi-head attention module, input embeddings are projected into three separate vector spaces: Queries (Q), Keys (K), and Values (V). Scaled dot-product attention computes attention weights as:
Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax((Q * K^T) / sqrt(d_k)) * V
where d_k is the dimensionality of the key vectors. The scaling factor sqrt(d_k) prevents the dot products from growing excessively large for high dimensions, which would otherwise push the softmax function into regions with tiny gradients.

2. Encoders vs. Decoders
Transformer models generally follow one of three architectural variants:
- Encoder-Only (e.g., BERT, RoBERTa): Designed for contextual language representation, classification, and token extraction. BERT uses masked language modeling (MLM) and next sentence prediction (NSP) during pre-training to build bidirectional context representations.
- Decoder-Only (e.g., GPT-3, LLaMA, Mistral): Designed for autoregressive text generation. These models utilize causal masking in their self-attention layers so that tokens can only attend to previous positions in the sequence.
- Encoder-Decoder (e.g., T5, BART): Well-suited for sequence-to-sequence translation, summarization, and text restructuring tasks.

3. Dense Vector Embeddings and Sentence Transformers
Standard word embeddings like Word2Vec and GloVe assign static vectors to individual tokens, losing context and polysemy. Sentence Transformers (such as all-MiniLM-L6-v2) produce fixed-size dense representations of entire sentences or passages. The all-MiniLM-L6-v2 model uses a 6-layer architecture with 384 hidden dimensions. It is trained using contrastive learning and distillation to place semantically similar sentences close together in vector space under cosine distance.
